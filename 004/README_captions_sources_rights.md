## Instagram caption (draft, not posted)

In 1990, the most advanced telescope ever launched sent home its first pictures. They were blurry.

Hubble's 2.4-metre mirror had been polished with incredible precision, to the wrong shape. The edge was off by about two microns, roughly a fiftieth of the width of a human hair, because a test instrument on the ground had been set up just over a millimetre out of place.

So in December 1993, seven astronauts flew up on Endeavour and, over five spacewalks, gave it glasses: corrective mirrors, some as small as a coin, and a new camera with the fix built in.

The mirror is still the wrong shape today.

| Mirror ground (1979) and polished (1981) at Perkin-Elmer | NASA captions 7995584, 8108126, 8109563 |
| STS-61: launched 2 Dec 1993 on Endeavour, 7 astronauts, 5 spacewalks | NASA SM1 page; NASA caption sts061-s-088 |
| COSTAR corrective mirrors, some as small as a US nickel; WFPC2 with built-in correction | NASA SM1 page |

| M100 WF/PC1 vs WFPC2 comparison | NASA image 9408670 (1994) |

"Almost every Hubble picture you've ever seen was taken through that correction" refers to the fact that every instrument installed since 1993 carries its own correction. The primary mirror itself was never replaced.
