## Instagram caption (draft, not posted)

On 14 February 1990, Voyager 1 was about 6 billion kilometres from home when it turned its camera around.

Carl Sagan had pushed for one last look back. It was risky: from out there, Earth sat close to the Sun, and the glare could damage the cameras.

Voyager took 60 frames, a family portrait of the planets. In one of them, a ray of sunlight scattered inside the camera. Sitting in that ray, by pure chance, is Earth: a crescent about 0.12 of a pixel.

34 minutes later, Voyager switched its cameras off to save power. They were never turned on again.
