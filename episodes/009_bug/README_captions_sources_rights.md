## Instagram caption (draft, not posted)

On 9 September 1947, at 15:45, the team testing Harvard's Mark II found something in Panel F, Relay #70: a moth, caught between the contacts.

They taped it into the logbook and wrote one line: "First actual case of bug being found."

The key word is "actual". Engineers had called glitches "bugs" for decades. In 1878 Thomas Edison wrote of "bugs", "as such little faults and difficulties are called". The note wasn't naming anything new. It was a joke: someone had finally found a literal one.

Grace Hopper, who worked on the Mark II, told the story for years and made it famous. The Smithsonian says the logbook was probably not hers, and no one knows for sure whose handwriting it is.

In 1988 the page sat in a Navy museum, labelled 1945. Today it is at the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History, with the moth still taped down.

**Cut or softened on purpose:**
- **"Hopper found the moth":** this is the myth. The narration never says she found it or wrote the line.
- **Whether Hopper was in the room:** the Jargon File says she was not present, while CHM says she was. Because the sources disagree, the script stays silent on it.
- **William "Bill" Burke wrote the entry:** some sources credit him, but we could not confirm it, so it is left out.
- **"The moth caused a failure":** the log says only "(moth) in relay", so the script says the team "found something".
- **"Debugging was coined here":** the NHHC caption implies this, but it is disputed (Shapiro/OED), so it is not used.
- **The 13,000-relay figure:** this comes from secondary sources only, so the narration says "thousands".
- **Display status at NMAH:** we could not confirm whether the logbook is on view, so the narration says only "at the Smithsonian".

**Verification note:** WebFetch and direct HTTP were blocked in this research session. The Smithsonian, NHHC, CHM and Commons pages were checked through their search-indexed text, not by opening them. Before publishing, open the NMAH object page once to confirm its wording.
