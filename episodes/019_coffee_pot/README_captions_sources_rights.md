## Instagram caption
This coffee pot should never have been watched by the world. ☕

In 1991, computer scientists at Cambridge pointed a camera at a coffee pot in the Computer Laboratory. Coffee club members on other floors kept climbing the stairs to find it empty.

The camera fed a frame grabber in an Acorn Archimedes, and a program put a small greyscale image of the pot on their screens. It updated about three times a minute.

In November 1993 the picture went onto the web. The camera was switched off on 22 August 2001.

That month a Krups coffee machine from the pot's later years sold on eBay for £3,350 to Spiegel Online. It was not the original pot. The coffee maker shown is a stand-in, and the lab photo is from 2005.

## Sources
- Built 1991 at the Cambridge Computer Laboratory by Quentin Stafford-Fraser and Paul Jardetzky; 128x128 greyscale camera, capture card in an Acorn Archimedes (Wikipedia 'Trojan Room coffee pot' citing Stafford-Fraser) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_Room_coffee_pot
- Reason: coffee club members on other floors climbed several flights of stairs, often wasted if the pot was already emptied; image updated ~3 times a minute, greyscale (Stafford-Fraser, The Trojan Room Coffee Pot (1995)) https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/coffee/qsf/coffee.html
