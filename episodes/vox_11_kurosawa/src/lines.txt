A rip-off of Akira Kurosawa's film
paid him handsomely.
In nineteen sixty-one,
he made Yojimbo.
A nameless samurai
plays two rival gangs
against each other.
Three years later,
Italian director Sergio Leone
made A Fistful of Dollars.
Same story,
but with cowboys.
Nobody had bought the rights.
Kurosawa's studio, Toho, sued.
Kurosawa himself reportedly wrote to Leone
that it was a very good movie,
but it was his movie.
The case settled out of court
before the film
reached American cinemas.
Reports say Kurosawa got
a share of worldwide takings.
The story goes it paid him
more than Yojimbo.
But Yojimbo had borrowed too.
Kurosawa reportedly named
Dashiell Hammett's The Glass Key
as a source.
Critics also hear
Hammett's novel Red Harvest in it.
So the copy was
a copy of a copy.
