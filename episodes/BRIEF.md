# @lost_index research brief (for each episode)

@lost_index is a TikTok/Instagram/YouTube Shorts channel of 45–60 s vertical mini-documentaries ("file no. 00X").
Tone: calm, precise, curious, lightly wry. One true, surprising, well-sourced story per video, ending on a quiet twist.
Captions on screen are lowercase typewriter lines; big italic serif words land on key beats ("risky." "forever." "is earth.").
Every fact must be checked against reliable sources (primary docs, museums, NASA/agency pages, major newspapers, academic sources).
Never invent quotes. If a popular version of the story is wrong or disputed, say so (that is often the twist).

Visuals = real archival images that are PUBLIC DOMAIN or CC (CC0 / CC BY / CC BY-SA ok, with attribution), plus original diagrams we draw.
Wikimedia Commons is the main source. For every image give the DIRECT file URL on upload.wikimedia.org
(use a ~1600px thumb URL: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/File.jpg/1600px-File.jpg — or the original if smaller),
the Commons file page URL, author, date, licence, and what it shows. Verify each URL pattern from the actual Commons file page (WebFetch it).
NASA images: images-assets.nasa.gov direct URLs are fine. Avoid anything "fair use", logos/trademarks as the hero, or images of living private people.

Narration: 120–140 words (≈ 45–55 s), 8–11 beats. Short sentences. Numbers written as they should be spoken.
Hook in the first sentence, under 3 seconds spoken: the surprising claim itself, not a scene-setter
("The first webcam watched a coffee pot." not "In 1991, at Cambridge..."). Beat 0 gets the strongest image and a
`big` headline (2–4 words) — both are on screen from frame one. Structure: hook → the moment → the turn → quiet ending line.
Cut context that doesn't serve the twist. Early data: retention falls off fast, and sound / "listen to this" stories do best.
Post captions end with one short question viewers can answer in a word or two ("Did you rewind your tapes? 👇").
