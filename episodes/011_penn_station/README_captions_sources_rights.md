## Instagram caption (draft, not posted)

Pennsylvania Station opened in 1910. Designed by McKim, Mead & White, its waiting room was inspired by the Baths of Caracalla in Rome, under a ceiling 150 feet high.

In the early 1960s the railroad agreed to tear it down so a new Madison Square Garden could be built on the site. On 2 August 1962 architects, Philip Johnson among them, picketed outside. It didn't help. Demolition began on 28 October 1963.

Two days later The New York Times wrote: "we will probably be judged not by the monuments we build but by those we have destroyed."

On 19 April 1965 Mayor Robert F. Wagner signed New York City's Landmarks Law and created the Landmarks Preservation Commission. In 1978 the U.S. Supreme Court upheld that law in Penn Central v. New York City, and a tower was kept off Grand Central Terminal.

And the old station? The Brooklyn Museum says Adolph Weinman's facade sculptures were carted to a landfill in the New Jersey Meadowlands along with the rest of the rubble. One figure, "Night", was retrieved and now stands in the museum's sculpture garden. How much of the rest still lies in the marsh, nobody can say for sure.

**Cut or softened on purpose:**
- **"Ada Louise Huxtable wrote 'Farewell to Penn Station'":** the editorial was unsigned. The script says only "The New York Times wrote".
- **"All of the rubble / every statue went to Secaucus":** not supported. Many of the 22 Weinman eagles survive elsewhere (Commons says about 18; e.g. Ringwood State Park NJ, the National Zoo, Hicksville station), one clock group is in Kansas City's Eagle Scout Memorial Fountain, and the Brooklyn Museum also holds a column section. The script says "much of it, statues included".
- **"Secaucus" by name:** sources agree on the Meadowlands; Secaucus is specific to CLUI and the Beyer Blinder Belle essay, so the narration says Meadowlands (the image label names Secaucus only as a place in the photo).
- **The NYT photograph of "Day" in a Secaucus landfill (Eddie Hausner):** good detail, but sources disagree on the year (1968 per Beyer Blinder Belle; 1966 per New York By Rail), so it is left out.
- **"The Landmarks Law saved Grand Central":** the ruling upheld the law and kept the tower off; the script says "protecting Grand Central".
- **Vincent Scully's "One entered the city like a god..."** line: not used (length), and not re-verified this session.
- **Eagles returning to the current station:** Vornado removed the two 7th Avenue eagles in 2019 for storage; current status unconfirmed, so not used.
