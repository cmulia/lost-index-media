George Lucas turned down
a raise for Star Wars.
Reportedly paid one hundred
fifty thousand dollars
to write and direct it,
he was urged to ask for more.
Instead, he asked Fox
for something vaguer.
The rights to any sequels,
and to the merchandise.
Fox, the story goes,
thought it had the better deal.
At Christmas nineteen seventy-seven,
the toys were not ready.
So the toymaker sold
an empty box with a certificate.
Shoppers bought it anyway.
By nineteen ninety-nine,
Fortune reported,
merchandise had earned
twice as much as the films.
Lucas kept those rights
thirty-five years.
In twenty twelve
he sold his company to Disney
for about four billion dollars.
His reason, he said:
Star Wars could live beyond him.
A raise is paid once.
A story, he bet,
keeps paying.
