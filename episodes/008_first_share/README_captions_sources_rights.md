## Instagram caption (draft, not posted)

In 2010, a history student working in the archive at Hoorn picked up a printed form from 1606. It turned out to be the oldest known share in the world. Except it isn't quite a share.

In March 1602, the charter of the Dutch East India Company, the VOC, said that anyone living in the land could buy in. In Amsterdam alone, 1,143 people did, among them a merchant's maid with 100 guilders.

In Enkhuizen, the town messenger Pieter Harmensz put in 150 guilders, about a year's pay, in instalments. When he paid the last one, on 9 September 1606, he was given a printed receipt, filled in by hand. The first dividends, from 1610, came mostly in spice: mace, pepper, nutmeg. Clerks noted his payouts on the paper until 1650.

The paper never proved he owned anything, though. A VOC share was a line in the company's capital book, and selling one meant a trip to the East India House, where a bookkeeper moved it from one account to another.

After his death, the receipt went into the town archive. It was unveiled on 9 September 2010, 404 years to the day. Today, European law requires exchange-traded shares to be held as book entries. Back to the ledger.

Wording choices:
- The narration says "the oldest share in the world" in the hook and immediately undercuts it. Captions and tags never call the paper a "share certificate". On screen it is the "voc receipt".
- The narration does not say "first company to sell shares" or "the first stock exchange". It says only what the charter said, and that the building was completed by 1611.

Cut because it could not be verified, or was disputed:
- **That Harmensz himself was paid in spices.** Only the company-level dividends are sourced.
- **A death year (1638) and the claim that he "never sold".** These come only from secondary summaries.
- **"Oldest stock exchange".** The Antwerp bourse is older as a commodity exchange.
- **A Leiden University Libraries blog post claiming a 27 Aug 1604 Enkhuizen transfer receipt.** It could not be confirmed. If it holds up, it would challenge the "oldest" label, so please check it before posting.
- **Bloomberg dates the find to February 2010.** The narration avoids the month.

Licences, authors and dimensions were confirmed through web-search reads of each Commons file page.
