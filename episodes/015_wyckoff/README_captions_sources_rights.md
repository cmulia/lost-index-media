## Instagram caption (draft, not posted)

The oldest house in New York City is a farmhouse in Brooklyn: the Wyckoff House, on Clarendon Road in East Flatbush. Its oldest room is dated to around 1652, when New York was still New Netherland.

It is named for Pieter Claesen, who came over in the 1630s as an indentured servant and could not read or write. He later ran a farm for Peter Stuyvesant, the Dutch governor, and became a wealthy magistrate. His family kept the house until 1901.

But he wasn't born a Wyckoff. "Claesen" just meant "son of Claes". After the English took over New Netherland in 1664, families needed surnames they could pass down, and Pieter chose Wyckoff.

In 1965 the house became Landmark No. 1 of New York City's new Landmarks Preservation Commission. At the hearing, a representative of the highways department asked for it to be moved to extend Ditmas Avenue. The borough historian objected.

It's still there, in a small park off a busy Brooklyn avenue: New York's oldest house, named for a man who had to choose his own name.

**Cut or softened on purpose:**
- **The tree-ring "twist":** no source we could reach gives the dendrochronology study's lab, year, sample count or felling dates. Wikipedia only lists 1652 as "dated via dendrochronology", and one aggregator says the tree rings *confirmed* 1652. There is no reliable evidence that the tree rings moved the date, so the script says only "is dated to around 1652" and the twist became the surname and the 1965 road request.
- **Who built the oldest room:** the LPC says the house was built before 1641, on land Wouter van Twiller bought c. 1636, and American Heritage mentions "a tiny house dating probably from 1638". Wikipedia says the family "moved in around 1652", and one family blog says the Wyckoffs only bought it in 1737. Because the sources disagree, the narration never says Pieter built it or owned it. It says only "it's named for" him and "his family kept the house".
- **Arrival year:** 1637 in most sources, 1636 in the LPC report and the Holland Society summary, and 1632 in one blog. The narration gives no year, and the date tag says "1630s".
- **Exact year of the surname:** the sources give 1664 (English takeover) or later (some say 1687). The narration says only "after the English took over". The 1664 on the timeline marks English rule, not the date of the name change.
- **Why "Wyckoff":** the museum suggests a farm near Marienhafe, while others say it means "town magistrate". This is disputed, so it is left out.
- **Late-1970s fire (NYPAP: 1978, set by vandals), the 1937 family association, the 1961 buy-back and the 1969 gift to the city:** all true, but cut for length.
- **"Never moved":** no source says outright that the house is on its original foundations, so the narration says only "it's still there". The LPC designated the house where it stood and argued it should not be moved.
- **Ditmas Avenue:** it is named in the IG caption, which quotes the LPC, but not in the narration. We did not check whether the road was later re-routed, so nothing is said about it.
- **The highways department "wanted it moved":** the LPC says "a representative of the Department of Highways asked that the house be moved".
- **Landmark dates:** the hearing was on 21 Sep 1965 and the designation on 14 Oct 1965 (LPC). NYPAP gives 21 Sep as the designation date. The on-screen tags use the LPC dates.

**Verification note:** WebFetch and direct HTTP to wikipedia.org, commons.wikimedia.org, nyc.gov, loc.gov, wyckoffmuseum.org and npgallery were all blocked in this research session (proxy 403 / DNS).
