Charlie Chaplin's coffin was stolen two months after burial. He died on Christmas Day, nineteen seventy-seven, and was buried in Corsier-sur-Vevey. On the second of March, nineteen seventy-eight, two men took the coffin from the grave. Weeks later, a caller demanded a ransom. Reports put it near six hundred thousand. His widow, Oona, refused to pay. Police tapped her phone and watched two hundred phone booths. In May, they arrested two refugee mechanics, one Polish, one Bulgarian. They led police to the coffin, buried in a cornfield near Lake Geneva. The family reburied him under concrete. The leader got four and a half years. The ransom was never paid. The only thing the crime changed was his grave. Would you have paid the ransom?
