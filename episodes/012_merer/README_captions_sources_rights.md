## Instagram caption (draft, not posted)

In 2013, archaeologists led by Pierre Tallet found rolls of papyrus at Wadi al-Jarf, an ancient harbour on Egypt's Red Sea coast. They are the oldest inscribed papyri yet found, from near the end of King Khufu's reign, about 2560 BC.

Several belong to an inspector named Merer, who ran a crew of boatmen. His logbook follows them loading white limestone at the Tura quarries and sailing it down the Nile to Giza, most likely for the Great Pyramid's smooth outer casing.

He wrote it all down in half-day steps: two or three round trips every ten days, where they sailed, what they carried, where they spent the night. He reported to Ankhhaf, the king's half-brother. Other sheets account for food and supplies for his men.

The diary alone can't tell us whether these men were free. But the workers' town and the builders' tombs excavated at Giza point the same way: organised, rotating crews, fed by the state. Probably not the slaves of legend, though not exactly volunteers either.

The king left the pyramid. The crew left the paperwork.

Would you have kept a work diary? 👇

**Cut or softened on purpose:**
- **Crew size:** the ministry (Times of Israel, 2016) says about 40 men, Smithsonian says about 200, and other accounts give 160. The narration says only "a crew of boatmen".
- **Year 26 vs 27:** sources disagree on the cattle-count reading, so the script says "c. 2560 BC" and "for King Khufu" without a regnal year.
- **Direct quotes from the logbook:** no verbatim published English translation could be opened from this session (Wikipedia's lines and Clement's rendering differ). Entries are paraphrased, with no quotation marks.
- **"Merer built the pyramid" / "the papyrus explains how it was built":** the log covers transport only, so the narration says "pyramid builder's logbook" and "cargo".
- **Slaves:** the diary does not state labour status. The narration says "This diary can't settle that" and leans on the Giza workers' town and tombs, hedged as "probably not slaves. Not exactly volunteers, either" (Lehner: obligatory, rotating labour).
- **"No blueprint survives":** this was a twist candidate, but we found no reliable source stating it, so it is not used.
- **Why the papyri ended up at Wadi al-Jarf** (the crew's later work at the Red Sea port) was left out for length.
- **Ankhhaf** was cut from the narration for length and kept in the IG caption. His bust photo was dropped because we could not confirm its licence.
