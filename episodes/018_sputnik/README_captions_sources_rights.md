## Instagram caption
This shiny ball held almost nothing but a radio, and it shouldn't have frightened anyone.

Sputnik 1 launched on 4 October 1957. It was a polished sphere about 58 cm wide, and its main payload was a radio sending beeps on 20 and 40 MHz that amateur radio operators could pick up.

Many people who looked up at a bright light in the sky were probably seeing the rocket's core stage, which was far brighter than the ball itself.

The beeps carried a little data too: their length changed if the temperature inside went out of range. On 26 October, after about three weeks, the batteries ran out and the signal stopped.

By then the alarm was in Washington. ARPA followed in February 1958 and NASA in July. One 1957 analyst found little sign of panic among the public.
