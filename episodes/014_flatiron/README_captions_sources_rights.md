## Instagram caption (draft, not posted)

The Flatiron Building was finished in 1902 on a wedge of land where Broadway crosses Fifth Avenue at 23rd Street. Officially it was the Fuller Building. Everyone called it the Flatiron.

Its prow is only about six and a half feet wide. When a north wind hit that point, it split around the building and whipped down to the sidewalk, and the corner got a reputation as the windiest in the city.

In October 1903 a Biograph cameraman filmed people at the foot of the Flatiron clutching their hats and skirts. An English visitor, Philip Burne-Jones, wrote in 1904 that loafers gathered on the curb to watch women's skirts blow about, and that police moved them on.

That, the story goes, is where "23 skidoo" comes from: police shooing men off 23rd Street.

Etymologists aren't so sure. "Twenty-three", meaning clear out, was already slang in 1899, three years before the building opened, and "skidoo" turns up in 1901. The wind was real. The slang just got there first.

Would you have said "23 skidoo"? 👇

**Cut or softened on purpose:**
- **"Burnham's Folly" / bets on how far the debris would fly:** Popik finds no "Burnham's Folly" for the Flatiron in the NYT archive, Google News Archive or Chronicling America; his earliest hit is a 1988 guidebook (the 1914 "Burnham's Folly" was a different, wooden tower in Connecticut). Cut entirely.
- **Bicycle messenger killed by the wind in 1903:** repeated by Wikipedia and blogs (one cites a 1903 Jackson Daily News "Wind Whirlpool" item), but no name, date or primary report was found. Cut.
- **Shop windows blown in:** only in the Burne-Jones passage as reproduced by a blog. Cut.
- **Stieglitz's "bow of a monster ocean steamer" and "That is the new America":** both come from his recollections, and sources give the wording differently. Not quoted; the Stieglitz photo is used only as a credited image.
- **The 1901 Edison film *What Happened on Twenty-third Street*:** a staged skirt-and-air-shaft gag; sources disagree on where it was filmed. Left out.
- **Billy B. Van, Dickens's *The Only Way*, telegraphers' code, the con-game theory:** these are competing origin theories, and none is settled. The narration says only "aren't so sure".
- **Height (285 ft vs 307 ft) and 21 vs 22 storeys:** the sources disagree, and the figures don't serve the twist. Not used.
- **"Twenty-three skidoo" itself before 1902:** the combined phrase is from 1906. Only the component "twenty-three" predates the building, and the narration says exactly that.
