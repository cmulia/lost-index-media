The War of the Worlds panic mostly didn't happen. On October thirtieth, nineteen thirty-eight, Orson Welles, aged twenty-three, aired a Martian invasion as fake news bulletins. Next morning, newspapers described a nation in terror. But that night, a telephone survey called five thousand households. Only two percent said they were hearing Welles. A rival comedy show was far more popular. So why the headlines? Radio was taking newspapers' advertising money, and papers had reason to call it unreliable. Some listeners were frightened. Historians say far fewer than reported. Still, the uproar carried his name to Hollywood. In nineteen thirty-nine, RKO gave him almost total creative control. His first finished film there was Citizen Kane. Maybe the newspapers' best invention was Orson Welles. Was it panic, or publicity?
