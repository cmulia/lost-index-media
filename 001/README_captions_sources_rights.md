# Listening File no. 001: Au clair de la lune, 9 April 1860

## Instagram caption (draft, not posted)

In 1860, Édouard-Léon Scott de Martinville recorded a voice as a line traced through soot on paper. His phonautograph was never designed to play anything back.

In 2008, researchers working with First Sounds turned scans of his sheets into sound. In 2009 they realised they had been playing it twice too fast. Slowed down, it's a man singing "Au clair de la lune", thought to be Scott himself.

Audio: First Sounds (firstsounds.org), CC BY. Excerpts from the 2008 release and Patrick Feaster's 2010 restoration, trimmed, faded and lightly filtered. Images: First Sounds publicity images (archive photos: Isabelle Trocheris); Scott's patent drawings and trace (INPI, via firstsounds.org); engraving from F. J. Pisko, 1865.
